# Reconcile overlapping findings

The coordinator reconciles branch results using the selected scope, observation cutoff, and frozen prior-change inventory. Keep observed effects and proposed remedies as distinct outputs.

## Match the findings

Group related findings by incident ID, prior change ID when applicable, and the version effective for the observed action. Check these identities against the sources before merging findings. Different versions or applicable conditions can explain apparently conflicting conclusions; retain those distinctions.

Within each group, separate observed facts, applicability and exposure, causal explanations, and remedy options. Link a new diagnosis concerning an earlier change to that change's record. Present related remedy options as one issue rather than duplicate proposals, while preserving their differences.

Complete when each overlap has attributable sources and any remaining identity uncertainty is explicit.

## Resolve evidence disagreements

For each material disagreement about facts, applicability, exposure, or causes, identify the conflicting claims and supporting source segments. Request a bounded reread or clarification from the relevant worker. In direct execution, inspect those segments yourself. Keep the follow-up within the selected work.

Decide from source support and comparison limits. Agreement among workers is not independent evidence. Preserve what both branches establish and record why another claim is unsupported, limited to different conditions, or still unresolved. Continue unrelated analysis while a clarification is pending.

Complete when each material disagreement has a supported resolution or a stated uncertainty with the missing evidence needed to resolve it.

## Compare remedies

Compare competing remedies only after identifying the facts and uncertainties they depend on. Consider the original expected effect, the conditions where the existing instruction helped or harmed, the behavior each option would change, verification needs, and added work or maintenance cost. An observation of harm can justify reconsideration without establishing that removal is the best remedy.

For example, removal and a narrower trigger can address the same excessive check. Compare the occasions where that check remained useful before choosing between them. Preserve applicable rules and explicit user preferences when formulating the options.

Recommend a concrete option when the evidence supports the choice. Give the rationale, tradeoffs, remaining uncertainty, and why the alternatives were not selected. If the choice depends on missing evidence or an unresolved user preference, retain the alternatives and state what would distinguish them. Distinct remedies may remain separate when they address different causes or conditions.

Complete when each issue has an evidence-backed recommendation or explicit alternatives with the missing evidence or decision identified. A combined heading alone does not resolve disagreement.

## Return the resolution

Return the matched incident and change references, agreed observations, material disagreements and their disposition, and the proposed remedy or open alternatives. Keep the assessment of the earlier change separate from the choice of a new remedy. The coordinator uses this result in the main skill's journal reporting and accepted-change steps.

Done when every overlapping issue has one traceable account, every material disagreement has a disposition, and the user can distinguish observed effects from proposed changes and unresolved choices.
